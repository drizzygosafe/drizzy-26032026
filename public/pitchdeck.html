What actually stops a learner

Nothing is missing. Everything is unreliable.

01

Availability

No car to learn on. No adult free to teach. No slot that fits a job or a college timetable. Most learners stall before the first session ever happens.

02

Trust

A stranger, a car, and a young woman — for a lot of families that is exactly where the conversation ends. Safety is the reason, not the excuse.

03

Standards

Price, syllabus and quality change from one street to the next. Ask anyone to name a driving school and they can't. There is no brand to trust.

This is not a service gap. It is an entire industry running without an operating standard — which is exactly why it can be organised.

Regulatory tailwind

In 2024 the law made private training centres a route to the licence.

From 1 June 2024, the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways allows a licence applicant to take the driving test at an accredited private training centre instead of a government RTO. Under the Motor Vehicles Act, the licensing authority may waive the test of competence for an applicant holding a certificate from a recognised centre.

What changed

The test stopped being a government monopoly. Accredited private centres can now assess and certify learners directly.

Why it matters to us

Whoever holds accreditation in a catchment becomes the default path to a licence for everyone in it — not just a place to practise.

What we're doing about it

Building the trainer density and SOP discipline that accreditation requires, so the qualification is a formality when we apply for it.

Source: Motor Vehicles Act 1988, Chapter 2 (morth.nic.in); MoRTH notification effective 1 June 2024, reported by The Hindu and Business Standard, May 2024.
